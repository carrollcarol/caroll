Introduction to English Springer Spaniels

The name English Springer Spaniel has been used since 1900. The English Springer Spaniel is a hearty, medium sized dog. Their tails are usually docked in American lines, and undocked in European lines. There is a major divide in the two types of springers: the field bred or working springer, and the show or bench bred springer.

Field bred springers are bred with the original purpose in mind, to hunt. There is not much room left for looks or even a standard. They are usually smaller and more compact than the show bred dog, with a much thinner, shorter, and coarse coat. The ears are smaller and less pendulous. They are dominantly white with a liver or black face and some ticking. This makes them easier to spot in the field. Field bred springers have more energy, making them more able to work. A well bred dog will be able to settle down in the home to save his energy for work, making them an excellent companion as well as a hunter.

History & Origin of the English Springer Spaniels

Going a long way back, history suggests the spaniel was first documented in Spain and was introduced thereafter to the Britons by Roman Legions. They were mentioned by name in ancient documents found in Wales dating back as early as 300 AD. The English Springer Spaniel was originally known as Norfolk spaniels until English dukes in the 16th and 17th century decided to split the litters between the smaller legged and longer legged dogs and breed them separately. The smaller spaniels would be known as “cocker” spaniels and used to hunt woodcock. The longer legged spaniels were used to flush game therefore rightly named “Springer” spaniels. It wasn’t until 1902 however until the Sporting Spaniel Society of Britain officially decided upon the name “Springer”.

When the Mayflower arrived in America in 1620 among the passengers were two dogs written about in historical documents. One was a Mastiff and the other an English Springer. Since their arrival to North America there may have been many Springers running around but the first English Springer Spaniel whose lineage was traceable was brought from England to Canada in 1913 with the desire to be breed. After only 15 years the English Springer Spaniel had grown to be among the most well-known and popular breeds to be registered for the American Kennel Club. During this time, the English Springer was breed very selectively to compete in conformation competitions (conformation refers to the structure and physical traits of a dog and how well they conform to the breed standard.

Today, an entire dog show may be referred to collectively as a conformation show.) Their durability and duality found them working long days in the field only to be entered into a conformation competition the next day. This impressive duality earned these types of springers the label “Dual type” springers and earned them much praise by the 1940’s when the last dual championship had taken place. At this point in English Springer history breeders began taking a more split approach where those dogs with outstanding qualities that lent themselves to the field were bred, while others with show qualities were bred for perfect symmetry, soundness, and beauty in terms of the breed standard. Today the English Springer remains as one of the most popular breeds worldwide both for their athletic prowess and loving attitudes.

Distinctive Physical Appearance

The English Springer Spaniel is a well-balanced, medium-sized sporting breed distinguished by its athletic build, graceful movement, and refined appearance. Bred for endurance and agility, the breed possesses a compact yet muscular body, allowing it to navigate dense terrain while hunting. Adult males typically stand 20 inches (51 cm) at the shoulder and weigh 50–55 pounds (23–25 kg), while females generally measure 19 inches (48 cm) and weigh 40–50 pounds (18–23 kg). Their broad skull, expressive oval-shaped eyes, and long, feathered ears contribute to the breed's intelligent and friendly expression.

The breed features a double coat consisting of a soft, dense undercoat and a medium-length outer coat that is straight or slightly wavy. This weather-resistant coat protects the dog from harsh outdoor conditions, making it well suited for fieldwork and water retrieval. Feathering is prominent on the ears, chest, legs, abdomen, and tail, giving the English Springer Spaniel its elegant and distinctive appearance. Regular brushing and routine grooming are essential to prevent tangles, reduce shedding, and maintain the coat's natural beauty.

English Springer Spaniels are best known for their striking bi-colored coat patterns, although tri-color variations also occur. The most common color combinations include liver and white and black and white, with some dogs displaying tan points above the eyes, on the cheeks, legs, and beneath the tail, creating a classic tri-color appearance. Coat markings are highly individual, ranging from large solid patches to heavily ticked or roan patterns, where colored hairs are evenly intermixed with white. This unique variation ensures that no two English Springer Spaniels have exactly the same coat pattern, making each dog visually distinctive while preserving the breed's characteristic elegance and charm.

Key Physical Characteristics

  • Medium-Sized Build: The English Springer Spaniel is a medium-sized breed with a strong, athletic frame designed for endurance, agility, and all-day field work.
  • Muscular Body: A well-developed, muscular body provides the strength and stamina needed for hunting, retrieving, and active outdoor lifestyles.
  • Expressive Eyes: Large, oval-shaped eyes in shades of hazel or dark brown give the breed its intelligent, gentle, and friendly expression.
  • Long, Feathered Ears: The ears are long, pendant-shaped, and covered with silky feathering, framing the face while enhancing the breed's elegant appearance.
  • Medium-Length Double Coat: A weather-resistant double coat protects the dog from cold, rain, and rough terrain while remaining soft and moderately easy to maintain.
  • Strong, Straight Legs: Well-boned front legs and powerful hindquarters provide excellent balance, speed, and agility across various terrains.
  • High-Set Tail: Traditionally docked in some countries (where permitted), or naturally left long, the tail is carried level with the back and wags enthusiastically, reflecting the breed's cheerful temperament.
  • Distinctive Coat Colors and Markings:Most English Springer Spaniels are black and white or liver and white, often with ticking, roaning, or tan points that create unique, eye-catching patterns.

Personality & Temperament

The English Springer Spaniel is an intelligent, affectionate, and energetic breed known for its friendly and eager-to-please temperament. Originally bred as a hunting companion, these dogs form strong bonds with their families and thrive on companionship, regular exercise, and mental stimulation. They are highly trainable, making them excellent candidates for obedience, agility, scent work, and other canine sports. English Springer Spaniels are typically gentle with children, sociable with other pets, and welcoming toward people when properly socialized. Their loyal, playful, and adaptable nature makes them exceptional family companions as well as reliable working dogs.

English Springer Spaniel Care Guide

Diet & Nutrition

A balanced, high-quality diet is essential for keeping an English Springer Spaniel healthy, energetic, and in peak physical condition. Proper nutrition supports muscle development, joint health, immune function, and a shiny coat while helping maintain a healthy weight throughout every stage of life. Feeding the right amount of nutrient-rich food, along with fresh water and appropriate treats, promotes long-term health and overall well-being.

Recommended Diet Options:

  • High-Quality Dry Kibble:A complete and balanced kibble formulated for active, medium-sized breeds provides essential protein, healthy fats, vitamins, and minerals for everyday health.
  • Wet (Canned) Dog Food: Wet food offers excellent hydration and palatability, making it a good option for picky eaters or dogs that need a softer diet.
  • Fresh Cooked Diet: Freshly prepared meals made with lean meats, vegetables, and whole grains can provide excellent nutrition when formulated to meet a dog's dietary needs.
  • Raw Diet (BARF): A carefully planned raw diet consisting of raw meat, bones, and organs may be suitable for some dogs but should be developed with veterinary guidance to ensure nutritional balance and food safety.
  • Mixed Feeding: Combining high-quality dry kibble with wet or fresh food can improve variety, enhance flavor, and increase moisture intake while maintaining balanced nutrition.

Always provide fresh water and monitor your puppy/dog weight, adjusting portions as needed to maintain ideal body condition.

Grooming Requirements

The English Springer Spaniel has a medium-length, feathered double coat that requires regular grooming to keep it healthy and tangle-free. Weekly brushing, routine ear cleaning, nail trimming, dental care, and occasional bathing help maintain the breed's appearance while supporting overall health and comfort.

Coat Care:

  • Brush the coat at least 2–3 times a week to remove loose hair, prevent tangles, and reduce shedding.
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks, or as needed, using a gentle dog shampoo to keep the coat clean and healthy.
  • Regularly trim the feathering around the ears, legs, paws, and tail to prevent matting and maintain a neat appearance.
  • Inspect the coat after walks or field adventures to remove burrs, dirt, grass seeds, and debris that can cause irritation or mats.

Other Grooming Needs:

  • Check and clean ears weekly
  • Trim nails every 3-4 weeks
  • Brush your dog's teeth several times a week dog-safe toothpaste
  • Regularly check the skin and paw pads for cuts, irritation and parasites after outdoor activities.

Start grooming routines when your puppy is young to help them become accustomed to the process. Most puppies enjoy the bonding time that comes with gentle grooming.

Exercise & Play

English Springer Spaniels are active, intelligent dogs that thrive on daily exercise, interactive play, and consistent training. Regular physical activity and mental stimulation help prevent boredom, support overall health, and encourage a well-behaved, happy companion.

Exercise Recommendations:

  • Daily Walks: 45–60 minute walk helps maintain physical fitness, burn energy, and provide mental stimulation.
  • Fetch: Playing fetch satisfies the breed's natural retrieving instincts while improving endurance and strengthening the bond with its owner.
  • Hiking: English Springer Spaniels enjoy exploring trails and varied terrain, making hiking an excellent full-body workout.
  • Swimming: Many Springer Spaniels love water, and swimming provides a low-impact exercise that's gentle on the joints while building muscle.
  • Agility Training: Obstacle courses and agility exercises challenge both the mind and body, improving coordination, confidence, and obedience.

They can be trained to walk on a harness and leash, providing additional exercise opportunities and mental stimulation.

Health Considerations

English Springer Spaniels are generally healthy dogs, but they can be prone to certain inherited conditions. Regular veterinary checkups, a balanced diet, routine exercise, and preventive healthcare are essential for maintaining their long-term health and quality of life.

Potential Health Concerns:

  • Hip Dysplasia: A hereditary condition in which the hip joint develops improperly, potentially leading to pain, stiffness, and arthritis over time.
  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy: An inherited eye disease that gradually damages the retina and can eventually lead to vision loss or blindness.
  • Ear Infections: Their long, floppy ears can trap moisture and debris, making regular ear cleaning important to help prevent infections.

Preventative Care:

  • Regular veterinary check-ups
  • Maintain a Balanced Diet
  • Provide Daily Exercise
  • Keep Up with Grooming
  • Keep vaccinations current

When purchasing a puppy, always work with a reputable breeder who tests their breeding dogs for genetic health issues. The average lifespan of a Springer Spaniel is 12-15 years with proper care.

The primary difference between a Springer Spaniel and a Cocker Spaniel is size: Springers are larger, taller, and heavier. Historically, Springers were bred to "spring" (flush out) larger game, while Cockers were bred to hunt smaller birds called woodcocks.

Springer spaniels get their name from their historical hunting style of "springing" or "flushing" game out of thick underbrush into the open. Centuries ago, spaniels from the same litter were categorized by size. The smaller dogs—which hunted woodcock—were called "Cockers," while the larger littermates—used to startle or "spring" hidden birds and small mammals for hunters—were called "Springers"
